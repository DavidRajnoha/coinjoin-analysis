How often JoinMarket coins go back into the mix

The FINDINGS.md analysis rerun on the 2026 Dumplings rescan, after recovering 19,734 JoinMarket coinjoins the pipeline had filed as ordinary spends. Most of the change from the 2025 numbers is better detection, not different behaviour, and several earlier conclusions reverse.

  • ReversedThe 2022–23 collapse in remixing was a detection artifact. Dumplings filed many coinjoins of those years under the wrong protocol (§1).
  • ReversedValue matters little: remix runs from 66% for the smallest coinjoins to 83% at 1–5 BTC, then drops above 20 BTC (§3).
  • ReversedBusier months do not leak more. The density effect fell from r = +0.65 to +0.24, and is negative for the both-sides signal (§5).
  • Almost every large coinjoin is traceable: 95% of ≥1 BTC coinjoins have at most one mixed output whose destination is unknown (§4).
  • The single-taker question is still open. How often the one untraced output really is the taker's needs ground truth from emulation (§7, §8).
Terms

Reading the numbers

Coinjoin, maker, taker
A JoinMarket coinjoin has one taker, who starts it and pays fees, and several makers, who supply liquidity for a fee. Each participant receives one output of the same value and usually one change output. Mainnet data does not say who is who.
Mixed output
One of the equal-value outputs. A coinjoin with n participants has n of them. Change outputs are not mixed.
Remix
An output spent as an input of another labeled JoinMarket coinjoin.
Labeled corpus
The transactions identified as JoinMarket coinjoins. "Delivered" is the 2026 Dumplings output as received. "Augmented" adds the coinjoins recovered in §1 and is used throughout unless noted.
Candidate
An unlabeled transaction that spends outputs of three or more different labeled coinjoins. Ordinary spending rarely does that, so candidates may be missed coinjoins. They set the upper bound of the remix rate.
All-but-one (abo)
Every mixed output of a coinjoin is traced onward into JoinMarket except exactly one. "Both sides abo" means the same holds for its inputs. The lone untraced coin is a natural guess for the taker's.
90-day window
Per-year rates count only outputs remixed within 90 days, so recent years are not penalized for having had less time.
Intervening JM txs
The number of JoinMarket coinjoins broadcast between a coinjoin and the last remix of its outputs. Unlike days, this unit transfers to an emulator running at a different speed.
§1

The data and its repairs

The 2026 rescan used the fixed Dumplings detector and found 6.5 times more JoinMarket coinjoins than the 2025 corpus. Two further problems in the pipeline turned up while explaining what remained, and one of them needed repair.

Problem 1: JoinMarket coinjoins hidden behind the Wasabi 1 rule

Dumplings checks for other coinjoin protocols before JoinMarket. Its Wasabi 1 rule is loose enough to catch large JoinMarket coinjoins once JoinMarket moved to native SegWit in 2021, and the damage then spreads to the next coinjoin in the chain.

Step 1 · scan

A JoinMarket coinjoin is labeled Wasabi 1

All native SegWit, ≥10 equal outputs near 0.1 BTC, ≥2 unique outputs. 2,144 JoinMarket coinjoins match.

Step 2 · scan

Its child coinjoins are demoted

A transaction spending a "Wasabi" output is marked as not-a-coinjoin and filed as a JoinMarket post-mix spend.

Step 3 · post-processing

Only the parents are restored

restore_false_positives_for_others moves the 2,140 mislabeled parents back, but copies their children as post-mix records without checking them again.

Of the hidden coinjoins from 2021 on, 97.1% spend an output of a Wasabi 1-shaped JoinMarket coinjoin, against 3.2% of labeled coinjoins from the same years. They are also almost all non-RBF (98%), while the rest of 2021+ JoinMarket is 92% RBF. That points to one specific group of users or software, but this is an association and has not been tested.

Repair. augment_corpus.py promotes every post-mix transaction that passes all of the following:

  • the fixed Dumplings JoinMarket rule;
  • the same parse_dumplings checks the corpus went through;
  • it is not itself shaped like a Wasabi or Samourai coinjoin.

Run to a fixed point, this adds 19,734 coinjoins (19,732 in the first pass, 2 in the second).

Problem 2: small early coinjoins tagged as false positives

43,798 delivered coinjoins carry fp_reason: MIN_SAME_VALUES_THRESHOLD but were left in the corpus. All have exactly 3 or 4 equal outputs: a taker plus 2 or 3 makers. The upstream check apparently ran with a minimum of 5. Early JoinMarket defaulted to 2 makers, and the only independent count from that period confirms these are real.

§2

Remix rate

84.6% of outputs are spent into another labeled coinjoin. Counting candidates as coinjoins raises that to 93.1%. The gap between the two bounds shrank from 28 to 8.5 percentage points, so the remaining uncertainty is small.

The upper bound barely depends on the candidate threshold

The upper bound now moves less than 4 points across thresholds. In the 2025 corpus it moved from 75% to 34%.

§3

Value is a weak covariate

In the 2025 corpus remixing rose fifteenfold from the smallest to the largest coinjoins. Most of that was missing labels: small coinjoins were the ones the old detector missed. Now the spread is about 1.25×. The rate rises up to about 1 BTC, stays flat to 20 BTC, and falls above.

The mix of sizes still shifts over time. Coinjoins below 0.5 BTC are 42–70% of 2015–2021 and 83–91% of 2022–23 and 2025–26 (65% in 2024). With remixing flat across sizes, this shift no longer moves the headline rate.

§4

Connectivity and the all-but-one pattern

For each coinjoin: how many of its mixed outputs are traced onward into JoinMarket, and how many of its inputs came from it? Candidates count as JoinMarket here.

3,502 coinjoins of ≥1 BTC leave exactly one output and exactly one input untraced (2026_augmented/both_sides_all_but_one.txt). That is the strongest single-taker signal the data offers. Its overall rate barely moved from 2025 (7.4% → 8.5%), but it no longer rises with value, and it is not stable over time.

§5

Traffic density no longer predicts leakage

The 2025 analysis found busier months leaked more, even after removing each year's trend. With better coverage that effect largely disappears. Busier months still trace slightly more outputs, but they show fewer both-sides all-but-one coinjoins, not more.

§6

What is still missing

  • ~500–800 coinjoins two or more hops behind the recovered ones. Their JoinMarket parents were all hidden, so Dumplings never recorded them. An API sample of 200 untraced outputs found 20% spent into JoinMarket-shaped transactions absent from the file. Closing this needs spend lookups (mempool.space, or an indexer on a full node) or a Dumplings rescan with the precedence fix.
  • 11,661 candidates (7% of the corpus) still absorb 8.5% of outputs. Before the repair, a third of candidates had no coinjoin shape at all. The remaining set is likely richer in such transactions, so the 93.1% upper bound is generous.
  • 1-maker coinjoins (about 2% of early JoinMarket) are excluded by design.
  • Not rerun: the structural check of candidates against mempool.space. The random-chain baseline (0 of 100) does not depend on the corpus. The corpus invariants all pass.

§8

Emulation calibration

How long until every mixed output of a coinjoin has been remixed? This sets how long an emulation must keep running after its burn-in, so that edge effects do not masquerade as untraced taker coins.

§9

Sensitivity: without the 3–4 party coinjoins

§1 shows the 43,979 tagged coinjoins are genuine early JoinMarket, so this is a robustness check, not an alternative estimate. Removing them turns their transactions into unlabeled spenders, which moves part of the remix share into candidates.
